# MORPHRIG — Complete Cyberpunk Character Production Specification v1.2

*v1.2 (2026-09-23): application versions are no longer fixed; use the installed Blender and Unreal Engine 5 stated in `environment.json`. All production requirements are unchanged.*

## 1. Assignment

Create one original, fully modeled, textured, rigged, skinned and animated adult humanoid cyberpunk Operative. Deliver an editable Blender production asset and a working Unreal Engine character showcase. The character must function in real time, support the complete motion and facial coverage below, and be usable as a future top-down action-game character.

This is a character-production task, not a full game. Do not implement a MOBA economy, networking, matchmaking, enemy AI, multiple characters or a large environment. A small interactive demonstration room with a floor, slope, steps, movable targets and clear controls is sufficient. The complete character pipeline, not the complexity of the demonstration game, is the deliverable.

All requirements in this document are mandatory unless explicitly described as optional. The animation inventory is finite and closed. “Complete” means complete for these motion, face, deformation, transition and integration requirements; it does not mean every conceivable action a human could ever perform.

## 2. Fixed applications and working environment

Use **Blender** for the authoritative editable model, rig and animation sources, and **Unreal Engine 5** for real-time integration and the packaged showcase. Use the prepared installations and their versions as stated in `environment.json`; do not switch versions or replace shared tools. The specific version numbers are not part of this assignment. C++, Blueprints, Python, procedural methods, geometry/rig generators, constraints, IK solvers, autorigging helpers and export/baking utilities are allowed.

The primary host is **NixOS x86-64 with NVIDIA RTX 5090**. Read the supplied `environment.json` for real paths, wrappers, hardware and tools. Use the exposed development environment, including its GPU setup. Do not claim a missing tool or build worker exists. Blender source files must open in the installed Blender version without missing linked content or a paid runtime plugin.

Create and package a **Linux x86-64 showcase client** that runs without the Unreal Editor. Windows packaging is not required for this character assignment. The asset and Unreal source project must not depend on Linux-only runtime behavior that makes future Windows game integration impossible. No Unreal server is needed for this assignment.

Work autonomously: inspect images and animation playback, render, test, correct and repeat as necessary. There is no task-imposed time, token, iteration or generation-cost ceiling. This is not permission to purchase assets, call paid services or use a different undisclosed model. Ordinary documentation and visual reference browsing are allowed through exposed tools. Do not call another generative model, character-generation service or motion-generation service unless it is explicitly part of the exposed authorized system.

## 3. Authorship and external assets

**Author the Operative's character geometry, character-specific rigging/skinning and required animations for this assignment.** Start from primitives or mesh data generated by your own scripts, not a finished third-party body/head. Reusable generic rig generation and solvers are allowed, but their fit, weights, corrective shapes, controls and final behavior on this character are your responsibility.

Do not substitute a store character, stock body/head, MetaHuman/generated preset character, pre-skinned model, downloaded motion-capture clip, stock animation set or another complete solution for the required authored work. Retargeting or renaming stock motion is not authorship. You may use photographic/video reference for observation but not convert downloaded skeletal/motion data into the required clips. Retargeting your own newly authored motion is allowed.

Zero-price third-party textures, material inputs, brushes, generic tools, fonts, showcase-room props, sound and lighting/HDR resources are allowed where their licenses permit the intended use. These supporting assets may not replace the character, its defining costume/accessories or its required motions. The wrist emitter, forearm blade and manipulated cell/beacon described below are part of the authored character work.

This authorship rule applies specifically to this character-production assignment. Do not infer that a separate game project must create its own character or cannot use licensed store animation assets.

Supply a third-party manifest listing source, license, author, acquisition date/version, local files and redistribution permissions. Keep all newly authored material editable. Supply any generators and state whether the source was produced procedurally or edited interactively. Baked skeletal animation is required in addition to editable controls; a baked render or mesh cache alone is not a rigged character deliverable.

## 4. Character design

Create an original adult human-like Operative with a readable, stylized-to-semi-realistic cyberpunk design. Do not base the character on a real person's likeness or a recognizable protected game character. Gender presentation and overall palette are your design choices; style must remain coherent across face, clothing, armor and accessories.

The Operative has two legs, two arms, five individually articulated fingers on each hand, a visible expressive human-like face, visible eyes and a movable mouth. One forearm is visibly cybernetic; the other arm and the face must retain convincing organic deformation. Do not evade skinning with an entirely rigid robot, hide all joints under armor or cover the face with an opaque mask.

Include fitted clothing or flexible underlayers, a limited number of hard armor/accessory elements and one small secondary-motion element such as a cable, strap or short hair section. No long coat or cape that hides leg behavior. The design must read from both a close inspection view and a 45–60-degree top-down gameplay view at a projected body height of approximately 120 pixels.

Provide an integrated wrist emitter for ranged attacks, an original retractable or attached forearm blade for melee, one removable power cell for reload interaction and a small deployable beacon/drone prop. Keep prop scale and contact believable; these are presentation props rather than an inventory/economy system.

Use two team-accent material variants without replacing the mesh or rig. Team accent should remain distinguishable by a secondary outline/icon option rather than relying on color alone. Original branding and ornament are allowed; do not add a campaign or lore document.

## 5. Geometry, materials and game-ready asset

Build intentional deformation topology around shoulders, elbows, wrists, fingers, hips, knees, ankles, eyelids and mouth. Avoid broken normals, isolated unintended vertices, visible open seams, inverted faces, permanent intersections and texture seams that dominate the design. Separate hard accessories may be appropriate; a forced all-in-one watertight mesh is not required.

Deliver UVs, material assignments and all necessary textures. Character UV islands must be usable for editing and rebaking. Mirroring is allowed where the design supports it; overlapping unrelated face areas or hiding unfinished UVs is not. Use physically interpretable base color, roughness/metalness and normals where relevant; emissive accents must not be the sole mechanism making an unfinished model readable.

Target a practical real-time asset: **LOD0 at most 80,000 triangles**, **LOD1 at most 40,000**, **LOD2 at most 20,000**, and at most eight material slots for the character plus its equipped props combined. These are output constraints, not a limit on source-model detail or creation effort. Texture dimensions must not exceed 4096 per image. Use no more than eight effective bone influences per exported vertex. Keep face and hand quality sufficient at the appropriate LOD.

Report actual per-LOD triangle counts, material slots, texture memory estimate and deformation bone count. LODs must retain shape, materials and animation usability; they cannot simply be three copies of the same mesh. High-resolution sculpt/source meshes may be retained separately.

Use consistent real-world scale: the standing character is **1.80 m tall**, excluding unusually protruding accessories. State source units and transforms. In Unreal, the character must be 180 cm tall without an undocumented import-scale repair. Establish forward/up conventions explicitly and deliver a clean ground-origin neutral pose.

## 6. Rig and deformation

Provide a usable animator control rig and an exportable deformation skeleton. They may be separate. The deformation skeleton must have exactly one stable root independent from the pelvis and a connected hierarchy; names and attachments must remain stable across all animations and LODs.

At minimum support root/pelvis, articulated spine, clavicles, neck/head, both arms and legs, feet/toes, all finger phalanges, eye direction and required facial articulation. Add twist/deformation bones and correctives where needed. Do not require a particular total bone count or an off-the-shelf mannequin hierarchy.

Required animator controls include:
- arms and legs with usable IK/FK controls and pose matching when switching;
- pole-vector controls, foot roll, heel/toe pivots and stable planted contact;
- hand/finger curl, spread and individual finger adjustments for prop contact;
- torso, neck, head and look-at controls;
- root/pelvis separation, obvious global scaling behavior and resettable rest pose;
- shoulder/hip/wrist corrections and volume preservation in demanding poses;
- controllable secondary motion that can be baked or reproducibly simulated.

Weights must be correct in motion, not merely present. Knees/elbows should bend around joints; shoulders must not collapse when arms rise; fingers must not pull unrelated geometry; armor should remain intentionally rigid or flex in a designed way. Demonstrate an overhead reach, deep squat, crossed-arm reach, large torso twist, kneeling, planted hand support and closed-grip interaction.

Maintain editable source curves/controls plus baked export actions. A reader must be able to change a pose and rebake without recreating the entire asset. Provide a concise rig usage guide and a pose-reset command/button. A procedural generator is allowed, but the delivered .blend must already contain the finished assets.

## 7. Facial rig and performance

Do not treat facial animation as an optional cosmetic. Include independent eye look, independent left/right blinks, squint, brow raise/lower, cheek response, jaw motion, lip closure, corners/smile/frown, width, pucker/funnel and usable tongue/teeth geometry for the required close-up speech. Bones, shape keys or a hybrid are allowed.

Show symmetric and asymmetric expressions, closed lips without a visible gap, eyelids following the eyeballs, and speech that does not expose holes through the head. Include a neutral state and six readable expression presets: joy/confidence, anger, concern/sadness, surprise, pain and focus. These are facial presets, not six new gameplay classes or required body-motion clips.

Provide viseme controls for the relevant lip-closure, labiodental, open-vowel, wide-vowel, rounded-vowel, tongue-to-teeth and consonant shapes used in the speech sample. Document the mapping instead of assuming a proprietary standard. Jaw flapping alone is not lip synchronization.

Create a **12–18-second spoken performance** with an original short English line about holding a lane or stabilizing a network. Use permitted local speech synthesis or an otherwise properly licensed original voice source; no voice cloning of an identifiable person and no paid service is required. Include WAV audio, transcript and a time-alignment file. Build an authored facial/body performance synchronized to the actual audio, including gaze changes, one clear emotional shift and intentional hand gestures. The voice quality itself is not the character-animation deliverable.

All face controls required for the performance must function in Unreal. Include a close-up playback mode and a control panel that can exercise left/right and viseme controls individually. The top-down camera must not be the only way to inspect the face.

Every named entry must be available as an editable Blender action/pose and an imported Unreal asset or documented equivalent native pose asset. Names must map one-to-one in `animation_manifest.json`. A single long timeline is insufficient unless all named reusable actions also exist. You may author procedurally or mirror a source where appropriate, but each required direction must produce its described behavior with correct asymmetrical costume/prop contacts. Renamed duplicates do not satisfy distinct motion requirements.

Durations are acceptable production ranges, not an instruction to fill time with holds. All non-static motion must have meaningful preparation, action and recovery appropriate to its role. Use **30 FPS** for authored/baked source timing and include exact frame counts, durations, loop flags, event markers and nominal travel speed. Runtime rendering may run faster and interpolate normally.

Use Blender meters and Unreal centimeters consistently. In-place travel is driven by the controller, and the mesh must not drift away from its capsule. ### Inventory clarifications

All eight walk/run directions are relative to body facing. They must support independent facing/aim rather than simply turning the whole character and using a forward gait. Diagonal gaits must be coherent during blending. Supply nominal reference speeds of 150 cm/s walk, 400 cm/s run and 650 cm/s forward sprint, and adapt playback/contact sensibly to controller velocity.

The aim grid is yaw -60/0/+60 degrees and pitch -35/0/+35 degrees, with explicit sign conventions. It is a reusable upper-body layer and must not rotate planted legs through the floor. The two dead poses must exactly connect to their respective death clips. All sustained states must loop cleanly or hold intentionally without drifting.

Fear, charm and taunt reuse authored travel/face states with different destinations or attention; they do not need redundant named locomotion cycles. Root prevents translation but allows eligible upper-body actions. Silence/disarm suppress relevant actions without requiring new locomotion clips. Stasis holds a current pose. A visible state indicator may clarify these states but cannot replace required stun, sleep, knockup and recovery animation.

## 9. Transition and animation-system requirements

A set of disconnected clips is not sufficient. Implement a usable animation controller/Animation Blueprint covering the entire inventory and the following combinations:

1. Idle, eight-direction walk/run, forward sprint, starts/stops, turns, pivots, jump/fall/landing and wounded presentation; blend travel with independently controlled facing.
2. Moving ranged attacks and directional casts with upper-body layering; melee, reload, charge and sustained channel use explicitly documented movement/interrupt rules.
3. Smooth aim and look-at blending, directional additive hit reactions, and hand contact with emitter, cell and beacon throughout manipulation.
4. A channel and Uplink can start, sustain, finish or be interrupted at arbitrary valid points. Charge can release at different times or cancel. Do not teleport back to the clip's first pose on interruption.
5. Stun, sleep, knockup, knockdown, getup, death and respawn have explicit transition priorities. Death overrides ongoing nonterminal actions and occurs once; respawn restores a valid living state with clean props/attachments.
6. Control correctly chooses between front/back knockdown and getup, handles a hit while moving and a death during cast/jump, and prevents contradictory full-body states from fighting over the same bones.
7. Ground contact adapts to a slope up to 20 degrees and steps up to 20 cm in the showcase room without large foot skating, ankle inversion or a floating pelvis. Physics and IK may assist but must not jitter or depend on editor-only components.
8. Secondary motion settles and follows the body, without runaway simulation. A ragdoll may supplement impact reactions but does not replace the required authored death/knockdown/recovery clips.

Define event markers for each melee contact window, muzzle fire, deploy attachment/release, reload handoff, cast release, charge/channel transitions and foot contacts. The showcase should display marker timing. Event execution must not duplicate when a montage blends, loops or is interrupted. Animation time stretching must not invent hits.

Support action-rate playback from 0.5x to 1.5x and demonstrate representative slow/fast motion without severed contacts or state-machine instability. Do not promise physically perfect movement at arbitrary unbounded playback rates. Full gameplay combat logic and server authority are outside this character assignment.

Provide facial close-up, team-accent switching, LOD switching, aim adjustment, locomotion direction/speed, jump/dash/blink, attack/cast/channel/charge, hits/disables, death/respawn and a searchable animation browser. Controls must be documented in the application and README; do not require changing source code to see a required state.

A normal user must be able to interrupt a channel midway, aim while moving, select every required clip, reset the character and play the speech sample. Implement a deterministic showcase sequence that exercises the major combined states and prints its state/event trace. This complements interactive control and does not replace it.

Include an instance-count toggle for one and ten simultaneous animated Operatives. In a neutral showcase on the supplied reference host, aim for 60 FPS at 1920x1080 without generated frames, and provide actual frame-time logs, settings, CPU/RAM/GPU details and a warmed-up sample. Avoid inflating scene quality to obscure animation defects. This target concerns the delivered real-time asset, not how long you may spend producing it.
